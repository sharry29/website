Thoughts on Some Books Read In (But Not Necessarily From) 2025

Negative Space by Lilly Dancyger

👍

I love a memoir about family. I love a memoir about growing up in New York. I love a memoir about experiencing art.

There's No Such Thing as an Easy Job by Kikuko Tsumura

👍

Quite a trick to combine fascinating magical powers with mindless drudgery. It's nice to feel that your work has an impact.

Fresh, Green Life by Sebastian Castillo

👍

An effete and delusional narrator. An invitation to a dubious party. A mention of expensive turkey sandwiches with cranberry miso mayo. A propulsive lead-up to a hilarious crashout. Everything you could need.

A Little Life by Hanya Yanagihara

👎

I made sounds like I was taking poison damage over time whilst reading this. This is miserable. Taught me the meaning of "hate read."

Lithium for Medea by Kate Braverman

👍

A worthy entry into the canon of Loser Lit. Braverman takes the whole "nothing applies" thing from Play It As It Lays and gives it as ammunition to an even more hopeless protagonist.

The Nenoquich by Henry Bean

👍

Another worthy entry into the canon of Loser Lit. Imagine a guy so miserable that having sex with him presents a risk to life and limb.

Willard Gibbs: The Whole is Simpler than its Parts by Muriel Rukeyser

👎

A fascinating premise: biography-as-poetry about an unknown genius from the 19th century. Many lovely ideas explored: the consequences of poor explanations, the importance of thinking with abstractions, the role of faith in science. And yet: an absolutely torturous slog. Oh well!

The Pale King by David Foster Wallace

👍👍👍

A messy set of parables about attention and focus and passion and effort and (self-)consciousness. This also includes the most jaw-dropping hundred page build-up to a pun I have encountered.

Lonesome Dove by Larry McMurtry

👍👍👍

I mean shut the fuck up how can anything be this good.

Thoughts on Some Games

🎮

Blue Prince

This fascinating game exhibits an enraging pattern: it is not always possible to do what you know that you need to do on each run of the game. Sometimes you draw bad cards and get blocked from making the connection you were looking for. This can happen at the end of a fairly long day that is otherwise spent going through the motions to hopefully get a good outcome. This is a feature I found maddening in The Outer Wilds, too, although that game managed to make up for it with a story that was inherently charming. There was nothing about the family in Blue Prince that was interesting enough to prevent me from feeling that my time was being wasted.

🎮

The Séance of Blake Manor

Lots of cool stories here. Playing this on the TV with the Steam Deck reminds me to be grateful for fast loading screens in other games on modern hardware. Boy howdy is it tedious to walk across the manor in this game. Nevertheless: it's sick.

Diceomancer

Challenging roguelike deckbuilder that does an excellent job of making you feel like you're breaking the game. Lovely hand-drawn art, too! You should play it.

🎮

Death Stranding 2: On the Beach

I haven't finished it so I don't have a ton to say except that it's so fun so cool so pretty so silly so great. Looking forward to another 40 hours at least.

🎮

Overwatch 2

This game is so good I'm sorry it just is.
